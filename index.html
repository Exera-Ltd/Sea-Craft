Our atelier

Ten minutes from the water, and it shows.

Sea Craft started on a veranda in Mahébourg with a bucket of shells and one very patient grandmother. Fifteen years on, six of us work out of the same street — cutting, sanding, setting and packing by hand.

We buy sand and shell only from licensed collectors, never from the reef. The driftwood finds us after a cyclone. Everything else is time.

How it's made

Three slow steps

Nothing here is moulded or machine-finished. That is the whole point, and also why we only ship so many pieces a week.

01

Gathered

Shells and sand from licensed island collectors. Teak and tamarind from fallen trees. Driftwood from whatever the sea returns after a storm.

02

Shaped

Cut, carved and sanded by hand at the bench. A frigate hull alone takes about ninety hours before a single sail goes on.

03

Sealed

Marine-grade resin, natural oils and a week of curing, so an island piece survives a Northern-European radiator.
